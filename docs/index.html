Why h33-h33-2-b2 is the family's best owner-reported file (0.2778), and whether we can beat it

owner-report 0.2778 (and the prompt's conflicting 0.3195 / 0.4413 leaderboard figures) is not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED in this repository; the leaderboard is login-gated and was not fetched. What is measured here, from SHA-256-pinned rasters (evidence/forensics_h33_vs_d28.json):

  • H19-5 solid = 121,131 line pixels → D2.8 = 44,090 binary dots on those same lines (NN spacing min √8 px) → H33 = the exact 37,654-dot subset deleting all 6,436 dots within ≤2 px of the catalogue. Owner-reported scores: 0.1922 → 0.2600 → 0.2778. The detector never changed — each step removed prediction mass the metric charges 0.2 per pixel for, where it could not earn kernel credit (300 m triangular kernel, max credit per truth pixel; catalogue pixels masked out of scoring).
  • Under DTI = TPw/(TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw) the optimum at fixed geometry is binary, and a dot must earn > 0.2·DTI ≈ 0.056 mean kernel credit to be worth adding. Thinning that field further is exhausted (mean dot credit ≈ 0.13 at 0.2778; reaching ~0.32 needs ~25% more credit per dot).
  • So beating 0.2778 needs a better ranking of pixels by true-fault probability, validated without leakage. That is what this repo's lanes keep testing — and the last four (feature appends, PU refit, missing-tooth, paired-flank step) all failed against the strike-steered control or duplicated its dots. This session's flank-step field detects steps well (its top feature beats every pointwise feature alone, AUC 0.6027) but localises lines too coarsely for the 3-px credit kernel.

Honest answer: not with this file, and not by perturbing H78-A. The strongest untried direction in the repo's records is a learned segmentation model (the organizer reference is U-Net class) trained on GPU and preregistered as its own lane.
