Why did h33-h33-2-b2 (GEMSDOE32) score 0.2778 — and can we beat it?

Scoring law (organizer-published; re-derived and unit-tested in vendor/gems57/metric.py): DTI = TPw/(TPw+0.2·FPw+0.8·FNw), 300 m triangular kernel. For binary dots, one more non-redundant dot raises DTI iff its kernel credit k > 0.2·DTI — at 0.2778 the bar is 0.0556 credit/dot. DTI is a budget: credit density at the emitted mass decides everything.

H33's ladder (all live scores = user-reported submission-page receipts): continuous parent h19-5 (multi-lineament-corroborated) 0.1922 → dotted d1.5 0.2477 → dotted d2.8 0.2600 → solo re-emit 0.2708 → +B=2 catalogue-flank prune 0.2778 (37,654 dots). Three multiplicative ingredients: parent credit density, kernel-matched ~2.8 px dotted spacing, and deleting zero-credit near-catalogue mass (the organiser masks mapped faults pixel-exactly). Full forensic ledger: docs/why-h33.md.

Beating it requires higher credit density in the top 37,654 cells — a better ranking, not a better thinning (the user-reported 0.4413 leaderboard top implies ≈59% more credit/dot than H33's parent). This lane contributes one measured ranking improvement: monogenic phase-congruency features raise holdout DTI by a significant paired +0.0121 over an identical 48-band stack without them, and lift the surface AUC vs withheld faults 0.674 → 0.696. That is a building block toward 0.4413, not a claim of reaching it.

This lane: H78-MPC — monogenic phase congruency (unique, not in any sibling)

Catalogue-missing blind faults have weak gradient amplitude — what HGM/tilt/worm/Blakely/Steger/radon lanes (all tried, live ≤0.16 as pure lanes) key on — but retain a coherent local-phase step across scales.
