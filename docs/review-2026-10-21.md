### Scientific interpretation and caveats

The official competition description says the evaluation faults are newly identified, manually labeled faults not in the existing public USGS fault database. The official metric is distance-weighted Tversky: α=0.2 (false positives), β=0.8 (false negatives), 300 m support, and a linear triangular distance kernel. That asymmetry rewards recovering missed target faults more than avoiding every false positive, while still penalizing off-target mass. A sparse dot field can earn credit when its locations are sufficiently near newly labeled faults. Excluding predictions near known faults can, in principle, reallocate a fixed prediction budget to off-catalogue candidates. Neither fact proves H33-2-B2's 0.2778 score was caused by its strategy; the causal claim requires controlled validation and a valid submission receipt.

The archive's projection is 0.274673, while the request reports 0.2778. The mismatch is an irregularity, not evidence that either value is wrong: the projection and organizer score are different quantities. The official page and receipt must be checked before calling the latter confirmed.
