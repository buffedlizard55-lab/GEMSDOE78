### 1.3 Why `H33` Reached `0.2778` — Four First-Principles Mechanisms
Under the official Distance-Tolerant Tversky Index ($\alpha = 0.2$, $\beta = 0.8$, triangular kernel $K(d) = \max(1 - d / 300\text{ m}, 0)$ on the $100\text{ m}$ `EPSG:32611` grid):

$$\mathrm{TP}_w(P) = \sum_{g \in G} \max_{x \in P} K(\|x - g\|_2), \qquad \mathrm{FP}_w(P) = \sum_{x \in P} \left[1 - \max_{g \in G} K(\|x - g\|_2)\right], \qquad \mathrm{DTI}(P) = \frac{\mathrm{TP}_w(P)}{\mathrm{TP}_w(P) + 0.2\,\mathrm{FP}_w(P) + 0.8\,\mathrm{FN}_w(P)}.$$

1. **Asymmetry of $\max$ vs $\sum$ (`H19-5: 0.1922 → D2.8: 0.2600`)**: $\mathrm{TP}_w$ applies a **pointwise $\max$** across predictions within $300\text{ m}$ of each truth cell $g$, saturating at $1$, whereas $\mathrm{FP}_w$ sums penalty **additively** over every positive pixel $x \in P$. Thinning a 3-pixel-wide ribbon (`121,131` px) into isolated dots spaced by $\approx 283\text{ m}$ (`44,090` px) eliminates $\sim 64\%$ of false-positive denominator mass while preserving almost all coverage along detected fault strands.
2. **Catalogue-Halo False-Positive Pruning (`D2.8: 0.2600 → H33: 0.2778`)**: Mapped fault traces (`existing_faults.tif`, $60,988$ pixels across $3,199$ components) are masked pixel-exactly out of scoring, yet their physical scarps and potential-field gradients span $100\text{–}300\text{ m}$ laterally across adjacent unmasked pixels. Detectors trained on mapped faults assign their very highest scores to the $1\text{–}2\text{ px}$ flanks of **already-mapped** faults, earning zero credit wherever no unmapped splay exists. Pruning the $6,436$ flank dots (`H33`) removes unproductive $\mathrm{FP}_w$ mass without reducing off-catalogue recall. On our controlled hide-and-recover evaluator (`evidence/h78_run_card.json`), widening flank exclusion from `2 px` to `3 px` raised `H78-A` `HOLDOUT-DTI` from `0.1586` to `0.1826` (`+0.0240`, paired 95% CI `[+0.0196, +0.0281]`).
3. **Sub-100m Physical Resolution in `H19-5`/`H33` vs Coarse 100 m in `H78-A`**: `H19-5` incorporated USGS 3DEP 1 m LiDAR scarp derivatives, topographic openness, GeoDAWN radiometrics, and thermal spring proximity, whereas `H78-A` (`0.1826` holdout) used only the 100 m 19-band raster smoothed at $150\text{–}300\text{ m}$, washing out narrow ($0.5\text{–}3\text{ m}$ throw) Holocene/Late-Pleistocene piedmont scarps.
4. **Why `H33` (`0.2778`) Is Still Far Below the Competition Ceiling (`0.3195`–`0.4413`) — Diagnosis of Three Structural Bottlenecks**:
   - **Bottleneck A — Positive-Unlabeled Self-Suppression from In-Sample GPS Memorization**: In `H78-A` (and standard tree classifiers on the 19-band stack), every unmapped fault is labelled $y = 0$ in the training set while smooth long-wavelength bands (`geod_shearrate`, `geod_dilaterate`, `geod_2ndinv`, `deq_n100a15`, `ieq_n100a15`, raw `tmi`, raw `iso_grav_anom`, raw `det_elev`) uniquely fingerprint every $10\text{ km}$ valley in Nevada. A depth-63 tree trained in-sample memorizes valleys lacking mapped faults and actively suppresses $\widehat{p}(x) \to 0$ right on unmapped faults! **Spatial-block out-of-fold cross-fitting** on high-pass translation-equivariant features completely eliminates in-sample suppression.
   - **Bottleneck B — Binary 1-Pixel Target vs Continuous 300 m Kernel Target ($Y_K(x) = \max_{g \in G_{\text{vis}}} K(x, g)$)**: USGS 1:24k/1:100k fault polylines have $\pm 100\text{–}200\text{ m}$ horizontal digitization offset relative to true physical 1 m LiDAR / magnetic scarps. Training binary $y \in \{0, 1\}$ punishes pixels at $d = 100\text{ m}$ ($K = 0.67$) as negative errors, whereas training **out-of-fold conditional kernel-credit regression** directly on $Y_K(x) \in [0, 1]$ estimates the exact Bayes expected self-credit $\widehat{q}(x) = \mathbb{E}[\max_{g \in G} K(x, g) \mid X]$ required by the DTI equation.
